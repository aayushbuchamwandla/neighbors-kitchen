# Neighbor's Kitchen — 3-Minute Demo Script

**For:** YC application "Demo" upload
**Live site:** https://yc-beige.vercel.app
**Total runtime:** 3:00. Screen recording + voiceover, no slides, no talking-head.

This script is pure product walkthrough. It doesn't repeat what you're already saying in the application text fields (what the company does, the market, competitors, business model) — that's covered there. The video's only job is to prove the product is real, works, and that trust is designed into every screen, not bolted on.

Record at 1920×1080, cursor visible, deliberate pacing — don't rush the verification badges or the cook story, those are the two moments that actually differentiate this from a delivery app.

---

## Full script — read word for word

Read this straight through at a normal, confident speaking pace (roughly 140–150 words/minute). It's ~430 words, timed to land at 3:00 including the pauses noted in brackets. Practice it aloud twice before recording — the brackets are where you stop talking and let the screen do the work.

> Delivery apps solved logistics. They never solved trust. This is Neighbor's Kitchen — home-cooked meals from a verified cook down the street, picked up in person. Every dish you're about to see is real, and so is every cook.
>
> **[0:15 — scroll the home page: Neighborhood Favorites, Trending This Week, Popular Cuisines]**
>
> On the home page, neighbors browse real dishes from real cooks nearby. Favorites people keep reordering. What's trending this week. Cuisines from fifteen different countries. Nothing here was picked by a delivery algorithm — the neighborhood picked it.
>
> **[0:35 — click into Discover, apply a cuisine filter, toggle "Available today"]**
>
> On the Discover page, every one of these photos is a real photo of the real dish. Filter by cuisine, by dietary need, by what's available for pickup today — and the results update instantly, because this is a fully working product, not a mockup.
>
> **[1:00 — open a meal, scroll down to Meet Your Cook and the verification badges — hold here, this is the most important beat]**
>
> Here's the part that actually matters. Every cook on Neighbor's Kitchen carries seven verification checks — government ID, food handler certification, an in-person kitchen inspection, a background check, phone and email verification, and neighborhood trust built up over real orders. This is the moat. It's the reason a stranger's home-cooked meal feels safe enough to order in the first place.
>
> **[1:45 — click Reserve Meal, set quantity, add a note, confirm, show the success screen]**
>
> Reserving a meal takes two taps — pick a quantity, add a pickup note, confirm. No delivery fee, because delivery isn't the point yet. Pickup is what keeps this simple, personal, and trustworthy from day one.
>
> **[2:15 — open a full cook profile, scroll to stats and reviews]**
>
> Every cook has a real story behind their food, real stats on repeat customers and meals sold, and real reviews from real neighbors. This isn't a star rating bolted onto a delivery listing — it's a person you'd actually want to buy dinner from.
>
> **[2:35 — open Become a Cook, scroll to the five-step timeline]**
>
> And it's two-sided. Any home cook can apply, get verified, and start selling to their own neighborhood in five steps.
>
> **[2:50 — back to the home page, scroll to Coming Soon, hold on the hero]**
>
> Trust comes first. Everything else — delivery, subscriptions, group orders, catering — comes after. This is Neighbor's Kitchen.

## Recording notes

- **Screen recorder:** QuickTime (Mac, free, File → New Screen Recording) or OBS Studio (Windows/Mac, free) both work fine at this length.
- **Cursor movement:** move deliberately, pause 1–2 seconds after each click before talking over the new screen — jump cuts read as broken software.
- **Voiceover:** record a scratch take reading the script above cold, time it, then re-record — first takes usually run 15–20% long.
- **No zoom-ins needed:** the site is responsive and legible at 1080p; don't add editing effects, YC reviewers watch hundreds of these and plainness reads as confidence.
- **File limit:** YC caps this upload at 100MB / 3 minutes — export as H.264 MP4, ~1080p, and it'll land well under the cap even at 3:00.

## Cut list if you're running over

If a full take runs past 3:00, cut in this order (least to most costly):
1. Trim the Discover-page filter demo to one filter, not two.
2. Shorten the Become a Cook section to a 5-second scroll-past, no pause on the timeline.
3. Never cut the verification badges beat (1:00–1:45) — that's the moment that makes this pitch land.
